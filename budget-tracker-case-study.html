01 / THE PROBLEM

The reporting problem

A project lead has planned budgets, exported actual spending, and several project owners. Without consistent categories and monthly reporting, overspends can remain hidden inside large spreadsheets.

02 / THE WORKFLOW

How the tracker turns data into decisions

  1. 01

    Separate editable inputs

    Keep project names, periods, categories, budgets and actual amounts in traceable source tables, instead of hard-coding dashboard totals.

  2. 02

    Calculate variance consistently

    Compute variance as actual minus budget, and percentage variance as that difference divided by the budget where the budget is nonzero.

  3. 03

    Flag and explain exceptions

    Show lines above an adjustable overspend threshold, roll results up by project and month, and include a reconciliation check on the summary.

03 / VERIFY THE FILE

What to verify in the sample

The sample covers six months and 90 budget lines across three fictional projects. Its dashboard compares budget and actual values with variance in dollars and percentages and highlights overspends.

  • Change a budget or actual amount and confirm the summary recalculates.
  • Inspect the overspend tolerance and test a category above the limit.
  • Compare the per-project roll-up with the combined overview.

Limitations and honest scope
